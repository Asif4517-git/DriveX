DRIVEX — OPEN-SOURCE FOUNDATION COMPARISON
Research date: 2026-11-06. Research and testing only; no DriveX game built.
Repository: https://github.com/Asif4517-git/DriveX

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — ONE RECOMMENDATION

Recommend OPTION B: LibreCorsa (https://github.com/KhazP/librecorsa) as the
provisional main CODE foundation, subject to a browser/hardware go/no-go test
and removal or clearance of incompatible assets. It has the strongest
inspected combination of 3D car rigging, per-wheel simulation, circuit
physics, race systems and a static browser build. It is NOT already an
open-world game and is NOT proven playable on the target PC. Starting from it
still requires a substantial open-world and road-vehicle expansion.

For open-world design, study Overdrive City's tile/collision/lane/traffic
code, not its arcade vehicle solver. For race organization and garage/save
patterns, study CarRace selectively. Integrating any of these is future work,
not performed here. The common engine alone does not make modules compatible.

No inspected project satisfies the entire vision today. No interactive game,
3D rendering, user steering, camera, collision, completed player race or FPS
was verified in this environment. Build and headless test success is narrower
than browser gameplay. Recommendation is conditional, not a claim that the
game is playable or commercially cleared.

3. SOURCE-CODE FINDINGS AND MODE COMPATIBILITY

The source statements below are inspection evidence, not proof of visual quality
or that controls work in an interactive browser. Full inspection of every file
was not possible. Lack of a discovered feature is not proof of absolute absence.

A. LIBRECORSA — circuit-first, best foundation
https://github.com/KhazP/librecorsa | Demo https://librecorsa.org/
MIT code; last push 2026-11-06. TypeScript/Next.js/React/Three WebGL2.
- src/lib/sim/f12026-car/VehiclePhysics.ts implements a 3D rigid-body model,
  four wheel/contact states, steering, braking, suspension, tire slip,
  drivetrain and fixed steps; the source describes 500 Hz outer and 4 x 2 kHz
  substeps. TrackColliderQuery.ts makes vertical ground queries against tagged
  track triangles indexed in an XZ grid; sample-ground fallback also exists.
  This is code-level evidence of a real vehicle solver, not independent proof
  of realism. LibreCorsa has multiple car implementations: choose/adapt ONE
  vehicle solver for DriveX rather than treating all of them as one system.
- src/lib/ported-car-model.ts loads glTF via createGltfLoader and maps named
  WHEEL_* and STEER nodes with per-wheel pivots; car rig is real mesh geometry,
  not an image or decorative-only asset. Actual rig behavior unplayed.
- F12026CarSim.ts implements sectors/lap counts; ai-opponents.ts has an
  opponent field with steering/throttle/brake and race clock. Lap recorder and
  ghost-store exist; localStorage, IndexedDB and vehicle setup paths are in
  source. README describes practice, time trial, AI racing, weather, multiple
  circuits and P2P, but qualification/championship coverage was not proven.
- Track-specific height/track progress, surface and barriers need adapters for
  streamed cities, arbitrary junctions, bridges, ground layers, parked cars and
  traffic. GroundQuery is a useful seam, not a ready-made open-world engine.
  The city must supply road/terrain contact, obstacle collision and AI lanes;
  its vehicle data must support road cars and all modes. Share one rig schema,
  input, garage, audio, camera and save service between scenes; refactor them
  out of simulator-specific UI where necessary. Rally needs loose surfaces and
  terrain; drag requires staging, timing, manual shifting and dedicated tuning.

B. OVERDRIVE CITY — strongest inspected open-world architecture
https://github.com/appleweiping/overdrive-city
Demo https://appleweiping.github.io/overdrive-city/ | MIT; pushed 2026-10-23.
JavaScript/Three r160. main.js creates WebGLRenderer, city and Traffic.
city.js uses one seeded tile map for geometry, collisions and lanes; config.js
sets 96 x 96 tiles at 8 m = 768 m square. It is a small explorable city, NOT
a Kerala-scale streaming world. traffic.js spawns/despawns cars, selects turns
and probes for cars/pedestrians. peds.js and daynight.js exist; source inspection
confirmed the day/night directional light and traffic implementation, not all
pedestrian behavior. README says no traffic lights; roads are right-hand traffic,
so a Kerala left-hand road model needs reworking.
vehicle.js uses box/cylinder procedural car meshes and an arcade forward/lateral
velocity model, not independently simulated wheels or realistic suspension.
It provides car/wall collision logic, but is not the desired car model or solver.
citymesh.js uses instancing for buildings, road markings, lamps and trees; its
comment estimates ~10 city draw calls, not a measurement. A single fixed map
and global instances are not streaming. localStorage holds best-run score and
mute, not a full save/garage. Track racing, checkpoints, race AI, improved
vehicle physics/rigs and road types would need major new work. Some traffic
logic could be adapted to an abstract road graph, with vehicle control commands
sent to the chosen solver; cannot be dropped into LibreCorsa unchanged.

C. CARRACE — compact racing/game-flow reference
https://github.com/alexander-uspenskiy/CarRace
Demo https://alexander-uspenskiy.github.io/CarRace/ | MIT; pushed 2026-10-15.
JavaScript/Three WebGL; src/sim/carPhysics.js explicitly implements a 120 Hz
2-axle bicycle model, not a fully independent 3D suspension solver. carStep.js
samples track-relative wheel surfaces; render/carModel.js builds a geometric 3D
Formula car with wheels and MeshStandardMaterial, not a high-fidelity imported
road car. raceDirector.js handles phase, lap crossings and finishes; AI code,
garage screen, upgrades and a versioned save store are present. Unit tests pass,
but browser experience was not verified. Imported car rigs, open-world terrain,
realistic full vehicle physics, and city traffic need major changes. Good race
state machine/save design candidate, not the authoritative vehicle solver.

D. NURBURGRING DRIVE — track-focused physics alternative
https://github.com/esc5221/drive-game | Demo https://drive-game.pages.dev/
Code MIT; pushed 2026-07-10. JavaScript, Three, Vite. js/physics.js constructs
six-degree-of-freedom vehicle state with four wheel records, 240 Hz stepping,
suspension, combined-slip tire curve, engine, manual/auto shifts and surface
weather grip. js/car.js uses GLTFLoader/DRACO and splits or positions wheel
meshes. js/main.js connects track, physics, camera, settings and ghost; code
and headless launch harness demonstrate vehicle calculation, not actual browser
driveability. Physics depends directly on Track queries, track distance and
wall assumptions; a general city ground/collision interface and traffic system
would be necessary. Track/ghost foundation is useful; AI race and championship
coverage unverified. Mixed licenses and real brand usage make assets risky.

E. RAYCAST RC CAR — small vehicle component/reference
https://github.com/icurtis1/raycast-vehicle
Demo https://raycast-rc-car.netlify.app/ | MIT; pushed 2026-07-06.
JavaScript, Three and cannon-es. src/Vehicle.js creates a CANNON.Body chassis,
corner spheres and RaycastVehicle with four suspension ray wheels; controls
call setSteeringValue, applyEngineForce and setBrake. GLB body and four wheels
are loaded separately. World.js loads a level GLB and creates static trimesh
colliders. This is a physics-linked 3D visual, not just an image, but physics is
explicitly arcade and visuals/controls were not browser-tested. No city/race
architecture was established. Add streaming, traffic, road cars, tracks, race
rules and improved powertrain. Assets' provenance not established: do not copy.

F. RACING GAMES — tested lightweight procedural circuit prototype
https://github.com/monon98/racing-games
Demo https://monon98.github.io/racing-games/ | MIT; pushed 2026-11-04.
TypeScript/Vite/Three/cannon-es. src/physics/vehicle.ts creates a rigid chassis
and RaycastVehicle; actual setSteeringValue, applyEngineForce, setBrake and
wheel-transform calls inspected. Tests exercise contact, forward motion and
steering headlessly. createCar.ts uses primitive box/cylinder 3D meshes, not
realistic cars. track/generator.ts and game/lapProgress.ts provide track/lap
logic; storage uses IndexedDB and localStorage. GLB import/export concerns
tracks, not proof of a completed imported-car pipeline. No city/traffic or
opponent AI verified. Both open-world and realistic vehicles require major work;
stronger candidate for a small circuit prototype than the full vision.

5. BROWSER, DEPLOYMENT AND HARDWARE

All six shortlisted projects target web technology in their inspected source;
none was independently rendered or played in a browser. Required WebGL2
availability on the actual GT 710, driver/Chrome/Edge combination is UNVERIFIED;
GPU variants and driver support differ. WebGPU must be optional with a genuine
WebGL fallback, not a requirement. All candidates require further HTTPS, keyboard,
controller, loading, save and runtime validation. Source contains keyboard
handling, but no key input was exercised interactively. Do not equate localhost
HTTP with real deployment or functional game.

Pages readiness — NOT deployed/tested:
- LibreCorsa next.config.ts uses output: export and configurable basePath /
  assetPrefix; build with /DriveX succeeded. Current official workflow deploys
  to Cloudflare and uses GitHub Pages for a redirect. Asset paths, service
  worker, routing, all files and optional online services need real subpath
  testing; optional leaderboard/signaling should not be required for solo play.
- Racing Games vite.config.ts uses relative base locally and repository base
  in GitHub Actions; .github/workflows/deploy.yml uploads dist to Pages.
  Production build tested without GITHUB_REPOSITORY; actual Pages unverified.
- Nurburgring Drive Vite config uses base './'; CarRace and Overdrive City use
  static files; RC defaults need subdirectory asset-path verification. Relative
  assets and import maps alone do not establish successful Pages deployment.
- LibreCorsa uncompressed output measured 355 MiB (du), public 351 MiB; two
  raw track files were about 26.7 MB and 26.3 MB. The upstream Cloudflare job
  compresses assets and moves files over 25 MiB to R2; those steps were not
  run. GitHub Pages hosting limits, deployment artifact limits, bandwidth and
  every asset's runtime path must be reviewed before selecting packaging. Do
  not mistake total build size for a single initial download.

Performance: no FPS, draw-call capture, polygon counts, texture VRAM or memory
profiles measured. The target GT 710 may struggle with high resolution, heavy
shadows, reflections, postprocessing and many cars; do not promise a frame rate.
Source evidence: City uses InstancedMesh and 2048 shadow map, RC desktop shadow
map 4096 and ~7 MB body plus four ~3.3–3.5 MB wheel GLBs in build output;
Racing Games tests assert instanced barriers/environment; LibreCorsa has
scenery LOD/instances, asset compression scripts, shadow and reflection code
and a high-frequency physics budget. These are architecture clues, not proof of
adequate speed. Chunked LOD/instancing plus per-cell frustum/occlusion culling,
texture downscaling/compression, capped device pixel ratio, shadow and
reflection presets, AI budgets, worker scheduling and streamed cells are a
plausible optimization path, not implemented or validated. Measure CPU and GPU
frame times, draw calls, triangles, texture memory, download size, shader time,
load and peak memory on the specified PC before setting numeric budgets.

6. LICENSE AND ASSET DUE DILIGENCE

Not legal advice. MIT permits code modification and commercial redistribution
with copyright/license notice, but does not clear separately owned assets,
third-party code, logos or trademarks. Audit each actual file and dependency,
record author, URL, license/version, modifications and attribution, and get
legal review before distribution. Do not use ambiguous or restricted content.
- LibreCorsa LICENSE and CREDITS.md: code MIT; Three MIT; fonts OFL; credits
  say FA-26 and several circuit scans CC BY 4.0 (attribution required); AMG
  and BMW GT3 models CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (noncommercial and share-alike); many
  Assetto Corsa mod sounds have their authors' terms/unknown authors and are
  stated noncommercial. Synthesized fallback samples are project's own MIT
  according to credits. Do not reuse NC models or mod sounds for a commercial
  DriveX. Even CC BY models of real cars may raise branding/design issues.
- Nurburgring Drive LICENSE: code MIT; OSM-derived track data ODbL 1.0 with
  attribution and database share-alike obligations depending on use; Porsche
  GLB CC BY-SA 4.0, Hyundai GLB CC BY-NC 4.0 per README. Noncommercial fan
  project and real marks. Textures/other models must be checked individually.
  Engine sound generator is credited MIT; fonts OFL/Apache per README.
- Overdrive City: LICENSE MIT; vendored Three header MIT; README says code,
  art and sound original/procedural. No complete independent provenance audit;
  per-file textures/fonts/audio still require review before shipping.
- CarRace: LICENSE MIT and vendored Three MIT per README. Car geometry and
  sounds generated in code, but team names/liveries can implicate trademarks.
  Confirm all fonts, textures and third-party notices; do not assume all MIT.
- Raycast RC: code MIT; cannon-es and Three are MIT upstream. Separate GLBs
  and reflection texture are present; README does not establish clear
  individual source/license in the inspected lines. Treat as UNVERIFIED and
  replace/clear rather than infer that MIT code automatically licenses them.
- Racing Games: code MIT; Three/cannon-es MIT upstream; inspected source makes
  procedural car and audio. Check all imported/user-provided GLBs and textures
  separately. Do not assume exported/imported user data is licensed.
- Low-poly placeholder candidate: https://kenney.nl/assets/car-kit official
  page lists CC0. It is not a verified realistic car; inspect original pack and
  terms before use. No suitable realistic, fully cleared vehicle library was
  established. Kerala OSM-derived data would need ODbL attribution/database
  assessment; use a provenance ledger, not an assumed blanket license.

8. ARCHITECTURE OPTIONS AND WORK REMAINING

A. One project unchanged: least initial integration, but no candidate provides
Kerala-scale world AND proper racing AND cleared assets. LibreCorsa alone needs
a large city, traffic and road-car systems; City alone needs a new vehicle solver
and race framework. High extension cost either way.
B. LibreCorsa code base plus selective systems (recommended): preserve/adapt one
chosen LibreCorsa vehicle solver and its rig/track race systems. Study/adapt
City's MIT tile/lane graph, spawn logic and instanced scenery, replacing its
vehicle.js physics with a controller that sends throttle/steer/brake inputs to
the one chosen simulation. Study CarRace's MIT race flow and versioned save,
not its bicycle solver. Both City and CarRace use Three but versions, axes,
units, track data, entity lifecycles and timing differ. Refactor through stable
interfaces rather than copying wholesale. Do not reuse their assets or marks
without separate clearance. Moderate-to-high integration work and testing.
C. Lightweight custom shell plus selected libraries/components: cleaner APIs
and potentially leaner low-end performance; highest upfront implementation and
revalidation effort. Three and cannon-es are MIT, but replacing LibreCorsa's
solver with cannon-es trades realism work for simpler integration. Do not run
both on the same player car. Reconsider C if LibreCorsa cannot meet the target.

Suggested boundaries for B: mode/session manager chooses open-world/track/
rally/drag scene; shared VehicleDefinition and rig + one fixed-step physics
system consume normalized controls and a world contact query; track and streamed
city each implement surface and obstacle queries. Render interpolated state;
AI produces the same controls; shared garage/save/audio/camera services consume
vehicle state. A city traffic graph needs left-hand lanes, intersections,
signals, obstacle and pedestrian rules; track mode needs AI lines, checkpoint/
laps and race lifecycle. Add broadphase and chunk lifetime handling for large
maps, coordinate precision, crossings/bridges and collisions across chunks.
This is a proposed architecture, not code tested or implemented.

A realistic first vertical slice is one license-cleared rigged car, one small
Kerala-inspired road area with a few traffic agents and one simple circuit,
shared controls/physics/camera/save, hosted as a static HTTPS site. It still
needs original or properly licensed car/world/audio, track/city integration,
quality settings, correctness tests and target-PC browser testing. Rough order
of magnitude: several weeks to a few months for a small experienced team,
depending on asset availability and performance; no schedule guarantee. The
complete vision (large world, varied cars, weather, rally, drag, championships,
multiplayer and polished realism) is a much larger multi-person effort.

9. DIRECT ANSWERS / RISKS

1 Best overall: LibreCorsa, provisionally and for CODE, gated by real gameplay,
  GT 710 performance and a clean asset replacement plan.
2 Strongest physics: LibreCorsa, on inspected sophistication and selected
  headless suites; real-world fidelity and browser control remain unverified.
3 Strongest open-world architecture: Overdrive City, though its 768 m grid is
  small and its solver is arcade.
4 Strongest inspected circuit systems: LibreCorsa; CarRace is a simpler,
  well-tested race-flow reference. Championships/qualifying not proven.
5 Best browser compatibility: UNVERIFIED. Racing Games has the clearest
  inspected Pages workflow; build and HTTP success do not prove compatibility.
6 One existing project for complete vision: no, substantial engineering needed.
7 Hybrid: yes, as described in option B; only one authoritative player solver.
8 Reuse candidates: LibreCorsa code/rig/race; selectively adapt City's lane/
  traffic ideas and CarRace race-flow/save patterns after licensing review.
9 Rewrite/extend: world streaming, Kerala road graph and traffic rules, road
  car data/rigs, city collision adapters, asset pipeline, garage, mode integration
  and optimization; do not retain City's arcade solver for the player.
10 Risks: no browser/hardware proof; 500 Hz CPU cost; track assumptions;
  physics/coordinate incompatibility; GT 710 WebGL2/driver unknown; asset and
  brand rights, ODbL/share-alike and noncommercial assets; static path/size.
11 Prototype work: see vertical slice above; significant integration and testing
  remain. No verified browser-playable DriveX currently exists.
